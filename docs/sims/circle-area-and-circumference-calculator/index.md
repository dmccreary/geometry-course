## About This MicroSim

A circle is drawn with its radius (red) and diameter (dashed green). As the radius changes, the panels on the right substitute the current value into the two circle formulas:

- **Circumference:** $C = 2\pi r$
- **Area:** $A = \pi r^2$

The third panel answers a question that students often get wrong: what happens when the radius doubles? The circumference doubles, because $r$ appears to the first power. The area becomes four times as large, because $r$ is squared.

## How to Use

1. Drag the **Radius** slider from 2 cm to 20 cm and watch the circle and both calculations change.
2. Use the **r = 5**, **r = 10**, and **r = 15** buttons to jump to common values and compare the results.
3. Read the **If the radius doubles** panel and check its numbers by moving the slider to twice the current radius.

## Lesson Plan

### Grade Level
9-12 (High School Geometry)

### Duration
10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites
Radius and diameter of a circle, the meaning of π, and squaring a number.

### Bloom's Taxonomy Level
Apply (L3)

### Learning Objective
Students will be able to calculate the circumference and area of a circle from its radius and analyze how a change in radius affects each measurement.

### Activities

1. **Predict** (3 min): Before touching the slider, students predict the circumference and area for r = 5 and r = 10, then check with the preset buttons.
2. **Calculate** (5 min): Students choose three radii, compute C and A by hand, and compare with the Measurements panel.
3. **Analyze** (5 min): Students record C and A for r = 4, 8, and 16 in a table and describe the pattern in each column.

### Assessment
Students find the circumference and area of a circle with a radius the MicroSim cannot show (for example r = 25 cm) and explain, without recalculating, how the area compares with the area at r = 12.5 cm.
